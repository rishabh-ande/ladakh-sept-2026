The plan

Eleven days, three early starts, one fixed day in Leh that doubles as your insurance.

02SEP · WED

Leh — arrive and do nothing

10 km in townsleep 3,500 mmax 3,600 m
  • 08:00 land at Kushok Bakula Rimpochee. Take an airport taxi; rental bikes cannot be collected airside.
  • Lie flat until about 13:00. No shower, no stairs, no smoking. Four litres of water between now and bed.
  • 14:00, walking slowly: Main Bazaar, Jama Masjid, Central Asian Museum.
  • 16:00 collect the five bikes and do a proper handover — brakes, chain slack, tyre tread, tool kit, spare tube, tow rope, jerry can. Test ride three kilometres in town and nothing more.
  • 17:30 Shanti Stupa for sunset. Ride up. Do not climb the 500 steps today.
  • Buy tonight: five oxygen cans, five 5-litre jerry cans, spare clutch and accelerator cables, spark plugs, dry fruit.
  • Lights out 22:00. This is the one night you go to bed early.
The real risk of this tripFlying straight from sea level to 3,500 m is the single most dangerous thing you will do, and it happens on day one. The 2nd and the 3rd exist only to earn Khardung La on the 4th.
03SEP · THU

Sham Valley — deliberately downhill

130 km6 h with stopssleep 3,500 mall tarmacdrops to 2,950 m
Elevation 2,950 m low · 3,560 m high
Surface · 130 km 130 km tarmac
Fuel Pumps at Leh, Nimmu and Saspol. Nothing to worry about. Longest dry run 60 km
Leh → Hall of Fame 4 → Spituk 8 → Gurudwara Pathar Sahib 25 → Magnetic Hill 30 → Sangam 35 → Basgo 40 → Likir 52 → Leh 130

This is the most important day of the trip and the one that looks like a throwaway. You landed yesterday at 3,500 m with roughly two-thirds of the oxygen you are built for. Your body spends the next 48 hours doing two things: breathing faster and deeper without you noticing, and dumping bicarbonate through your kidneys so your blood chemistry stops fighting that new breathing rate. Neither finishes overnight, and neither happens faster because you rested in bed.

What does help is moving gently at a lower altitude and then returning to sleep high. Sham Valley is the only direction out of Leh that goes down: the road follows the Indus west and loses 550 m by Sangam. You get a full day of easy exercise in thicker air, then come back and sleep at 3,500 m, which is exactly the stimulus you want. Riding east or south instead would take you up, and Khardung La the next morning would be a gamble rather than a plan.

How the day runs

  • 08:30Hall of Fame (km 4). The Army's museum of the Kargil and Siachen operations. Captured Pakistani kit, letters home, and the Siachen gallery with the actual clothing and rations used at 6,000 m. It will change how you look at the soldiers you pass all week. Allow 45 minutes.
  • 09:45Spituk Gompa (km 8). 15th-century Gelugpa monastery on a bluff over the Indus, right at the end of the airport runway. Climb to the Vajrabhairava shrine for the view down the valley. 30 minutes.
  • 10:45Gurudwara Pathar Sahib (km 25). Built by the Army in 1517 around a boulder said to carry the imprint of Guru Nanak, thrown at him by a demon and softened to wax on impact. Free langar, and by local custom every vehicle stops. Cover your head. 30 minutes.
  • 11:30Magnetic Hill (km 30). Put the bike in neutral on the marked patch and it appears to roll uphill. It is a gravity-hill illusion: the horizon is obscured, so a downhill slope reads as an upslope. Fun for ten minutes, and it is on the way.
  • 12:00Sangam (km 35), the lowest point of your first four days at 2,950 m. The Zanskar arrives green-grey and the Indus brown, and in early September they run side by side without mixing for a hundred metres before merging. Rafting operators launch from here. Eat lunch at the dhabas by the confluence — this long, low stop is doing real physiological work, so do not rush it. 75 minutes.
  • 13:45Basgo (km 40). Ruined mud-brick fort and citadel, capital of Lower Ladakh until the 1600s and the site of a three-year Mongol siege. Two 16th-century Maitreya temples survive inside, with wall paintings that won a UNESCO conservation award. Almost every tour van drives past it. 45 minutes.
  • 14:45Likir Gompa (km 52). Founded 1065, Gelugpa, with a 23 m gold-faced Maitreya sitting outdoors above the valley. The small museum holds thangkas and old costumes. Turn around here. 45 minutes.
  • 16:00Ride back to Leh, arriving around 17:15. Do not push the pace — the point today is time in the saddle, not speed.
  • 18:00Leh Palace, then up to Namgyal Tsemo Gompa for sunset over the Indus valley and the Stok range. Ride up; the walking route is 200 m of vertical you do not need today.

The other job: shake the bikes down

Tonight is the last time you are near a mechanic, a spares shop and a phone signal all at once. After 130 km on a bike you met yesterday, check every one of the five before dinner and get anything doubtful fixed in Leh this evening.

  • Chain slack and lubrication after a full day of dust.
  • Brake feel front and rear, and pad thickness — you will be descending Khardung La tomorrow.
  • Tyre pressures and a proper look at the tread and sidewalls.
  • Headlight, tail light, both indicators, horn. You will ride in the dark at some point.
  • Luggage mounts and bungee points under load, not empty.
  • Confirm every bike has its tool roll, spare tube and tow rope actually in it, not promised.
  • Fill all five jerry cans tonight, not at 07:00 tomorrow.
Decision pointIf anyone still has a headache tonight that paracetamol will not shift, or is nauseous, or slept badly and woke breathless, then the 4th slips by 24 hours and you repeat a low day. The 11 September buffer exists for exactly this, and using it costs you nothing at all. Going up anyway is how a good trip turns into an evacuation.

Standing rule

  • One 5-litre jerry can per bike, full, every time you leave Khalsar, Hanle or Nyoma.
  • A Himalayan 450 carries 17 litres. Plan on 350 km of usable range, not the 500 the spec sheet implies — low gears, rough surfaces and thin air all cost you.
  • Loose petrol is sold at Durbuk, Spangmik, Merak, Nyoma, Hanle and Karzok at a premium of ₹20–40 a litre. It is a fallback, never a plan.
  • Never let any bike in the group go below a quarter tank in Changthang. The group's range is the range of its emptiest bike.

Permits

This changed recently. If you read a 2023 blog, it will send you to the wrong office.

What you actually need in 2026

  • Indian nationals no longer need a separate Inner Line Permit. One Ladakh Environment & Health Fee receipt, bought online at lahdclehpermit.in, covers Nubra, Pangong, Chushul, Hanle, Umling La, Tso Moriri and Tso Kar.
  • Cost for an 11-day trip: ₹400 environment fee (one-time) + ₹20 per day wildlife fee × 11 + ₹50 Red Cross = about ₹670 per person. Valid three weeks.
  • Print twelve copies each. Checkposts keep one and there are more of them than you expect: South Pullu, Khardung La, Khalsar, Diskit, Turtuk, Thang, Durbuk, Tangtse, Chushul, Loma, Hanle, Sumdo, Karzok.
  • Also carry: original driving licence, twelve photocopies of Aadhaar or another photo ID, six passport photographs each, and the bike's RC, insurance and PUC.

If anyone in the group is not an Indian national

  • Foreign passport holders and OCI card holders need a Protected Area Permit through a registered Leh agent, in a group of at least two, and are not permitted beyond Man–Merak on Pangong.
  • That closes the entire Chushul → Tsaga La → Loma → Hanle leg, which means Umling La has to be reached from Leh via Chumathang instead, and the 7th disappears.
  • Tell me now if this applies to anyone and I will rebuild days 5 to 9 around Chang La and the Indus road.

Altitude

Read this part properly. It is the only section where being wrong costs someone their life.

Non-negotiable

  • 48 hours in Leh before anything high. Days 1 and 2 are that. They are not padding and they are not optional.
  • Four to five litres of water a day. No alcohol until you are back in Leh on the 10th. Never a sleeping pill at altitude — it suppresses the breathing reflex you are relying on.
  • Acetazolamide 125–250 mg twice daily, starting 24 hours before you go up. Get a doctor to sign off before you leave home: it is a sulpha drug and a diuretic, and it interacts with things.
  • Never sleep higher than last night if you already have symptoms. Descent is the only actual treatment; oxygen and drugs only buy you time to descend.
  • Headache plus nausea plus breathlessness at rest means go down, tonight, not in the morning.
  • Breathless lying still, a gurgling chest, or pink froth is HAPE. Confusion, or an inability to walk a straight line is HACE. Both are emergencies: descend immediately, put them on oxygen, and evacuate.
  • Medical points: SNM Hospital in Leh, and Army or civil aid posts at Diskit, Tangtse, Nyoma, Hanle and Karu. Emergency number 112.
  • Carry: five oxygen cans, Diamox, Dolo 650, twenty sachets of ORS, Avomine, Pantoprazole, Norflox-TZ, Betadine, a crepe bandage.

Kit, cash, signal

Bikes and spares

  • Five Himalayan 450s, roughly ₹2,500–3,500 a day. Only Ladakh-registered (LA) bikes may legally be rented for use here — check the RC before you pay a deposit.
  • Group kit: two tow ropes, two spare tubes, puncture kit, tyre levers, foot pump, spare clutch and accelerator cables, spark plugs, chain lube, one litre of engine oil, duct tape, cable ties, bungee straps, a headlamp each.
  • Two genuinely waterproof dry bags per bike. "Water resistant" means nothing at a nallah crossing.

On the body

  • Full-face helmet with a clear and a tinted visor, armoured jacket, knee guards, riding gloves plus waterproof over-gloves, boots that cover the ankle.
  • Two sets of merino thermals, fleece, down jacket, windproof and waterproof shell, rain pants, balaclava, buff, wool cap.
  • UV400 sunglasses, SPF 50 reapplied through the day, SPF lip balm. UV at 5,000 m burns you through cloud and through a visor.

Money and signal

  • ATMs work reliably only in Leh. Carry ₹20,000 cash each. UPI stops working past Leh and camps in Changthang want notes.
  • Prepaid SIMs from other circles do not work in Ladakh at all. You need postpaid. Jio and Airtel postpaid cover Leh, Nubra and patches of Pangong; get one BSNL postpaid in the group for Hanle and Changthang.
  • No signal whatsoever: the Shyok valley, Tsaga La, the whole Umling La road, most of Karzok. Agree a rendezvous point and a turnaround time before every leg.

Next 48 hours

You fly on the 2nd. These four things decide whether the plan survives contact.

  1. Book five Himalayan 450s. September is still peak season in Leh and five matching bikes go fast. Confirm they are LA-registered.
  2. Buy the environment fee at lahdclehpermit.in. Five minutes, about ₹670 each, then print twelve copies per person and put them in a waterproof pouch.
  3. Fix four nights. Thoise on the 4th and Hanle on the 8th hold. Rebook Sumur or Diskit for the 5th, move the Pangong booking from the 7th to the 6th, add Hanle for the 7th, and confirm the pending Karzok for the 9th. Hanle and Karzok are the two that will bite you — a few dozen beds each and no fallback within 100 km, so do those two first. Leh on the 2nd, 3rd, 10th, 11th and 12th is easy.
  4. Ask your rental agency to confirm two things: that the Chushul–Tsaga La road is open to civilians, and that the last stretch to Umling La is open. Both can shut at short notice and both have a clean fallback if you know in advance.
